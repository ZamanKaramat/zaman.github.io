Current work · 2026

What I'm working on now.

As a Research Associate at the Centre for Economic Research in Pakistan (CERP), I'm working across four research and policy projects, in collaboration with researchers at the University of Oxford, King's College London, the University of Bristol, and Princeton University.

I

Ongoing · Funded by the FCDO

Comparative Study of Evidence Use in Practice Across Reform Type: Revenue Policy in Pakistan

Asks how the type of reform (its scale, political salience, and institutional anchoring) shapes whether and how evidence is used in policymaking. Based in Pakistan, comparing federal and provincial revenue reforms such as tariff and property tax reform. It matters because existing work on evidence uptake is fragmented and offers little practical guidance on accounting for political and institutional factors.

Methods

Qualitative comparative case studies (2–3 tax reforms) using process tracing, 35–40 key informant interviews, and document and media analysis

PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATORS

Anam Shoaib, PI (Oxford & CERP) ; Nazish Afraz, PI (King's College); Zahra Mansoor, PI (University of Bristol)

Outputs

Final report, academic working paper, policy briefs, presentation deck, and a policy dialogue in Islamabad

II

ONGOING

Who Delivers the Evidence? Messenger Attributes, Credibility, and Evidence Adoption among Pakistani Civil Servants

Asks whether the messenger's rank, institution, role, gender, and nationality affect whether civil servants adopt policy evidence, apart from the evidence's quality. Based at the Civil Services Academy in Lahore with incoming officers before their first posting. It matters because evidence adoption is low even where evidence is strong, and no study has tested messenger attributes together.

Methods

Conjoint experiment and randomised paired-deliberation experiment with ~170 Grade 17 officers; repeated with the 2027 cohort

PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATORS

Anam Shoaib, PI (Oxford & CERP)

Outputs

Academic Publication

III

ONGOING

CERP x PolicyCopilot

Asks whether an AI evidence tool, PolicyCopilot, helps civil servants produce better-evidenced policy diagnoses without losing the ability to reason on their own. Based at the Civil Services Academy in Lahore, Pakistan. It matters because policymakers rarely use available evidence when designing programmes, and in low-budget settings like Pakistan poor programme design wastes scarce public spending.

Methods

RCT with 300–400 Civil Services Academy officers testing an AI policy-diagnostic tool, preceded by a ~200-officer pilot and followed by a team-level randomised extension

PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATORS

Anam Shoaib (CERP); Uzair Junaid (CERP); Shabeeh Haider Raza (CERP);  James Walsh (PolicyCopilot); Ghinwa Moujaes (PolicyCopilot); Tiffany McCormack (PolicyCopilot); Iris Chen (PolicyCopilot)

IV

Ongoing · Funded by Princeton University

Climate Finance in the Global South

Asks why clean energy investment in the Global South lags despite rising global capital commitments. Led from Princeton with CERP in Pakistan, covering the Global South broadly. It matters because climate goals depend on this investment, and the project looks at on-the-ground barriers beyond financial risk.

Methods

30–40 semi-structured interviews with clean energy financiers, developers, DFIs, regulators, and NGOs

Partners

Andlinger Center for Energy and the Environment at Princeton University and Centre For Economic Research in Pakistan (CERP)

Outputs

Policy-focused Research Paper
